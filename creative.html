TwoGather

A household budget management app, built for two.

My roommate and I kept household costs in Notes and did the math once a month — we always forgot something.

How it feels now

Whoever pays logs it and the balance updates instantly. It's saved us real time — no more sitting down once a month to do the math, and whoever owes more just buys the next round of groceries.

German Learning Companion

An interactive reading companion for my C1 exam.

Most German reading-practice tools either quiz you or just hand you a translation — neither actually teaches me to read.

How it feels now

My process: take an article, first learn the vocabulary, phrases and sentence structures, then retell it in my own words and build new sentences with what I just learned. This app matches that process exactly, and makes my knowledge gaps easy to see — so review is faster.

Main Quest

Daily side quests — email, admin, errands, meetings — keep crowding out what I actually meant to be doing.

What I'm building

A game-like system for life: my "main quests" are my real goals — job hunting means sending out CVs and getting through interviews, German means building vocabulary, and so on. Every milestone I clear in real life shows up as progress toward "beating the game." The point is to keep reminding myself what my main quest actually is right now — I'm still designing what else belongs in it.

My approach

Small friction → working prototype.

I build when a workflow feels unnecessarily heavy, when an idea keeps coming back, or when I want a tool that doesn't quite exist in the form I need. AI makes experimentation cheap enough to be part of everyday life — not a separate "coding project," just how I solve things now.

Each project started as a real, personal need — not a tutorial exercise. For the thinking behind why I build this way, and how it connects to how I use AI at work, see the full How I Work with AI page.
